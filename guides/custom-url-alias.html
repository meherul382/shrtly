Custom URL Aliases Explained: How to Create Memorable Links

A URL alias is the short code that appears after a short-link domain. A custom alias lets you select a meaningful code instead of relying on a random one.

Why aliases matter

A readable alias can communicate a topic, campaign or destination at a glance. It can also be easier to remember when shared offline.

Random vs custom codes

Random codes are quick and require no naming decision. Custom codes can be more descriptive but must be available and follow the service's rules.

How to choose a good alias

Keep it short, readable and relevant. Avoid confusing spellings, excessive punctuation and names that could imply an affiliation you do not have.

Practical examples

An article might use an alias related to its topic, while a campaign could use a concise campaign name. Avoid putting sensitive information into a public alias.

FAQ

Are aliases unique? A usable short code must be unique.

Can an alias contain spaces? Short URL systems commonly use URL-safe characters such as letters, numbers, hyphens or underscores.
